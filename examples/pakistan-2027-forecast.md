# Worked Example — Pakistan Security Forecast (Aug 2026)

A full run of the 5-panelist debate, captured end to end.

## Questions

- **Q1:** Will Pakistan's terrorism-related fatalities in 2027 be HIGHER than 2026?
- **Q2:** Will Pakistan experience state disintegration by Dec 31, 2030?
- **Q3:** If improvement comes, when does it begin (earliest quarter)?

## Scout Fact-Sheet (curated from PICSS, Balochistan Post, Pakistan Today, The Diplomat)

1. July 2026 = Pakistan's deadliest terror month this year; "terrorism out of control."
2. Balochistan overtook KP as most dangerous province; July most violent month there.
3. PICSS weekly (Jul 24-30): attacks 27→30; fatalities spiked to 54 with 36 injured.
4. Aug 22-23: Mastung convoy attack → curfew; bridge blasts; Panjgur assault.
5. Aug 21: security forces killed 49 militants in IBOs across KP + Balochistan.
6. TTP propaganda offensive intensifying (Noor Wali Mehsud video, Aug 5).
7. BLA pressuring CPEC/Gwadar corridor; Azad Kashmir unrest = emerging third front.
8. PM Shehbaz emphasized "grand dialogue" for Balochistan.
9. No real-money Polymarket market exists for Pakistan security — thin attention.

**Base rates:** Zarb-e-Azb = ~70% violence decline within 24 months but took 18+ months; deadliest-month shocks rarely sustain a full next year; no nuclear state has lost a province; 2025 was already near-record, 2026 beating it.

### What moved people (Round 2 excerpts)

- **Codex (43→49):** "Two fronts and repeated CPEC attacks constrain force concentration... A record 2026 also raises the threshold for 2027."
- **Claude (62→58):** "Peak years rarely persist is a fair instinct, but it assumes mean-reversion absent a structural driver; we're nowhere near single-front pressure."
- **DeepSeek (58→54):** "Mean reversion is real but selective... 2007-09 shows the opposite: consecutive peak years. The distinguisher is driver structure."
- **GLM (62→60):** "Qwen's dilution point trims me slightly, but the intelligence signal says escalation."
- **Qwen (52→55):** "GLM's propaganda lag is real and supports near-term momentum."
